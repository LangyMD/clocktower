The planned run separates generations, not neighbours

Precision grows with the square root of games played: halving the error costs four times as much. At ~$4,300 the ratings reliably separate models about 300–340 Elo apart. Separating neighbours a few months apart (~100 Elo) needs about 12 times the games.

Smallest gap between neighbours we can reliably detect

For each budget: the smallest difference between two neighbouring models in release order that the fit would call significant (95% confidence) in 4 runs out of 5. That comes to 2.8 standard errors of the difference. Lower is sharper. Ten models, round robin, side-swapped pairs, 12-player tables at ~$12 a game. The dashed lines are rough sizes of real gaps.

Where the gap sizes come from: in the v6 grid (100 games) the detection ratings were GPT-6 Astra 1837, GPT-6 Sol 1685, Opus 5.5 1560, Opus 5.5 at low effort 1305, Sonnet 5 1114. Those estimates carry ±150 of error themselves.

Why 6, 4 and 2 pairs, and why I'd drop it

The taper had two aims. Games between close models land near 50/50, and a near-even game carries the most information, since information per game is p(1−p). Close matchups also measure directly the neighbour gaps we care about. Distant matchups are lopsided, so each tells us less, but a few are needed to tie the top of the ladder to the bottom. Otherwise errors pile up along a chain. At the same number of games, computed for this table:

The taper is only about 4% sharper than a plain round robin. What matters is having cross-links rather than a chain, not how games are weighted across them. I now recommend the plain version: every pair of models plays 4 side-swapped pairs (360 games). It is a one-line rule, it checks every matchup equally, and it doesn't depend on guessing in advance which models are close.
