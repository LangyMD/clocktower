Why "within ten releases"

Two results from the literature pull in different directions. Rating error is governed by how well the matchup graph connects the models, which favours cross-links (Shah et al., JMLR 2016). And each game's information is p(1−p), which favours close matchups; that's the basis of Swiss pairing and Chatbot Arena's active sampling. Across 15 recent models the spread was narrow enough that the full round robin was as good as anything. Across 21 models and two and a half years, a strong good team beats a much weaker evil team almost every time, so those far-apart games carry little information. Simulated at about the same budget:

The band is about 10% sharper than the full round robin, with a quarter fewer games, and every model still meets opponents across a full year or more. The widest error bars sit at the two ends of the timeline: GPT-4o and GPT-6.1 Sol play only 20 games each, and GPT-4o's evil team will rarely win. That's what phase 2 is for.

Phase 2: top up where it matters

  • After phase 1, fit the ratings, then add side-swapped pairs to the matchups that most shrink the uncertainty we care about. Those are pairs of models close in rating with wide intervals, and models at the ends of the timeline.
  • Fix the rule and the budget before looking (for example, $2–3k in waves of 40 pairs). Allocate by uncertainty only, never by which way results lean, so the ratings stay unbiased.

Engine work before this can run

  • An OpenRouter backend for Opus 4.1 and Sonnet 4: they're only on Amazon Bedrock now. That needs an OpenRouter API key in the environment.
  • Models without reasoning (GPT-4o): no effort setting, so it plays with its plain replies.
  • Context limits: when a game outgrows a model's window (GPT-4o 128K, o1 and o3 200K, Opus 4.1 and Sonnet 4 200K), its oldest turns are dropped. The first message, with the seat's character and setup, is always kept.
  • GPT-5 needs your OpenAI organization verified. o1 and GPT-5 each need a smoke test and a memory check.
