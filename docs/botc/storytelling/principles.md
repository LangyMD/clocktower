# Storytelling principles

What experienced Storytellers aim for, summarised from community advice (the official wiki's Storyteller pages,
TPI's own guidance and long-running community discussions; see [../sources.md](../sources.md)). These principles
are the target behaviour for an AI Storyteller.

## The goal: a close, fair, memorable game

- **The Storyteller is not neutral about the outcome's shape, only about who wins.** The aim is a game that
  stays in doubt until the final day. When one team is running away with it, the Storyteller uses the discretion
  the rules give them (droisoned information, registrations, Mayor bounces, Tinker deaths, regurgitations) to pull
  it back, never by breaking rules.
- **Both teams must feel their choices mattered.** Don't decide the result through discretion; set up chances
  for the trailing team, and let the players take or miss them.
- **Fun beats balance at the margins.** Pick the more interesting legal option when two are equally balanced:
  information that creates a puzzle beats information that is merely noise.
- **The game must remain solvable.** False information should be explainable after the reveal. "In hindsight
  this all fits together" is the feeling to aim for.

## Rules of thumb

1. **Never lie outside what the rules allow.** You can only give false information when an ability is
   malfunctioning or the ability says so. You never lie about public game state (who died, vote counts).
2. **Stay consistent.** A drunk or poisoned player's information should follow one coherent false world across
   nights, unless the story demands a shift.
3. **Help the losing team, softly.** Small nudges each night are better than one dramatic reversal.
4. **Protect new players.** Give them clear information and generous rulings on madness; avoid making a new
   player the Drunk in their first game.
5. **Be neutral in tone.** Announce deaths and outcomes the same way every time; avoid tells (hesitation,
   emphasis) that reveal the Grimoire.
6. **Keep the game moving.** Time-box discussion and nominations; the game is long, and pace keeps it fun.
7. **Explain at the end.** The post-game Grimoire reveal, with the story of each decision, is part of the game.

## Balance levers, roughly in order of subtlety

1. What droisoned players learn (the most common and least visible lever).
2. How misregistering characters register (Spy, Recluse, Legion).
3. Choices in pair information (who the second player is).
4. The red herring and the Drunk's believed character (set once at setup).
5. Storyteller-chosen deaths (Mayor bounce, Gossip, Tinker, Pit-Hag Demon nights, Shabaloth regurgitation,
   Pacifist saves).
6. Setup choices (which characters go in the bag).

## Setting up the bag

- Pick characters that create a fun game for *this* table: their experience, the number of players, and the
  script's danger points.
- Avoid setups that are trivially solved (for example, a Baron game with only information roles that will
  reveal the Outsider count on night 1) or hopeless for good.
- Many Storytellers pick the Demon and Minions first, then Outsiders, then Townsfolk that create interesting
  interactions with the evil team.
- Random setups are fine for experienced groups and for an engine; good AI setups should still pass sanity
  checks (enough bluffs, no impossible jinx combinations).
