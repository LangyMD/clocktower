# Playing good

Strategy for good players, summarised from community advice (wiki strategy pages, community guides and
discussion; see [../sources.md](../sources.md)). Per-role tips are in each script's page.

## Core habits

- **Talk to everyone.** Information only helps if it reaches the people who can combine it. Spread private
  conversations across the table; silent players are hard to clear.
- **Share information, carefully.** Many groups share character and information early with trusted players and
  publicly later, to avoid painting a target for the Demon. Fragile roles (Ravenkeeper, Undertaker) can hide;
  public mechanics (Virgin, Slayer) are best claimed when you're ready to use them.
- **Think in worlds.** For each plausible Demon and Minion set, check which claims would be lies and whether the
  setup counts fit. A world that requires many coincidences is unlikely.
- **Remember droisoning.** Any single piece of information might be wrong. Prefer conclusions supported by two
  independent sources.
- **Execute every day** unless there is a specific reason not to (Mayor at three, a confirmed good on the block).
  The Demon kills each night regardless.
- **Use your dead vote wisely.** Dead players should save the ghost vote for the final days, where it often
  decides the game.
- **Final three**: the game usually comes down to one execution among three or four players. Lay out the worlds
  aloud; ask each finalist for their full story.

## Reading evil

- **Inconsistency over time** is the strongest tell: a claim that shifts, information that arrives late, or
  answers that don't fit the night order.
- **Too convenient**: claims that exactly answer the current suspicion.
- **Kill patterns**: who the Demon chooses to kill tells you who the Demon fears and who it wants to frame.
- **Votes**: evil players often protect each other on votes, especially late.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a single result as proof.
- Executing confirmed Outsiders who are dangerous to execute (Saint, Klutz) without checking.
- Leaving information unshared until death (dead players can still talk, but they can't vote much).
- Ignoring the Outsider count.
