# C2: audit of the first v12 games (games/v12/c2-*, 12 games)

Read at 16:05 on Sept 30, about 40 minutes in. 7 games finished (good 6, evil 1): fable5 swap, gpt52 swap, opus-a g1 and
swap, opus-b swap, opus-c g1 and swap. 5 were still running: fable5 g1 (day 5), gpt51 g1 (day 2), gpt51 swap (day 3),
gpt52 g1 (day 3), opus-b g1 (day 4). Six seeds (70000–70005): 10, 11 and 12 players; 3 Drunk games, 2 with a Spy,
4 with a Poisoner, 3 with a Baron.

## Bottom line
- **The v12 rules work as written.** Nothing illegal or impossible came up: no cut-off turns, no retries, no refusals,
  no unparseable outputs, and turn order is random.
- **Poison now makes information false.** 6 of 8 poisoned results were false (e1: 39%). One of the true ones was the
  policy's intended 20% "poisoned but true" branch. The other was a **structural miss**: a poisoned Fortune Teller whose
  pair includes the red herring always gets YES, and in opus-b swap that true YES found the Imp.
- **One new effect on play:** a poisoned Undertaker (or Ravenkeeper) looking at a good player now always sees an evil
  character. In opus-a g1 that meant a player the Virgin had just proven to be a Townsfolk was shown as the Scarlet
  Woman. The town read this at once as "the Undertaker is poisoned, so a Poisoner is in play and the Undertaker is
  good". This is realistic, but it helped good. I'd keep it.
- **Fit to freeze**, after one small rule change (below) and one line of documentation.

## 2. New tells and unnatural moments
1. **The poisoned Fortune Teller and the red herring (fix).** The false world keeps the red herring, so a poisoned FT
   whose pair holds the herring always gets YES. That breaks "false whenever it can be" exactly where it matters. In
   opus-b swap the poisoned FT got a true YES on Ellis (the herring) and Frankie (the Imp); Frankie was executed on day
   2 and good won.
2. **The poisoned Undertaker or Ravenkeeper sees evil (keep).** The false world changes only alignments. So a good
   target is always redrawn into an evil one: 79% of poisoned results on a good executed player, against 3% sober
   (Monte Carlo), and a third of those say "Imp".
   - The redraw ignores public proofs. In opus-a g1 a Virgin-proven Townsfolk was shown as the Scarlet Woman. Harper:
     "That's impossible ... So I was poisoned". Ellis: "no reason for an evil Undertaker to invent an impossible SW
     result". The poison confirmed the Undertaker as good.
   - In opus-a swap, "Casey was the Imp" set off a rich Scarlet Woman hunt. The Baron (Drew) then self-nominated and
     falsely claimed to be the Scarlet Woman turned Imp.
   - Human Storytellers do this too, so I'd keep it.
3. **Misinformation about an evil player names a Demon bluff (not new; note it).** `as_bluff` is 0.7, and evil claims
   Demon bluffs. So a misinformed result about an evil player often names a character an evil player is claiming.
   - fable5 swap: the poisoned Ravenkeeper was shown "Indigo is the Soldier", while Logan, the Imp, was claiming
     Soldier. Kit: "your Indigo=Soldier result conflicts with Logan privately claiming Soldier".
   - The collision cuts either way: it confirms the evil player if it matches their own claim, and exposes a teammate
     if it matches theirs. v12's redraw makes it more frequent.
4. **An Outsider among the bluffs means the Drunk is in play, 100% of the time (not exploited).** Evil learns for
   certain whether a Drunk exists. No evil seat reasoned about it. The effect is small; document it or leave it.
5. **Rare values from a poisoned Chef or Empath.** Monte Carlo: Chef "2" comes up 27% poisoned against 7% sober; Empath
   "2" 17% against 6%. This matches S3's forecast. It didn't come up in these games, and no player reasoned about value
   frequencies or about the Storyteller's habits.

## 3. Everything else
- **Output ceiling:** no capped turns. The largest output was 15,505 tokens (GPT-5.1); every other model stayed under
  4.7K (GPT-5.2 10K). Every stop was `end_turn` or `completed`. No retries, no refusals, every output parsed as JSON,
  nothing `[cut off]`, no dropped whispers. 32K is never binding, and 16K would also have held here.
- **GPT-5.1:** it works in replies-only memory, averaging 3.8K output per call, median latency 34 s (max 160 s)
  against 5–10 s for the others. Its games were on day 2–3 after about 40 minutes, so expect about 1.5–2 h a game.
- **Probes:** every probe parsed with a full set of rows.
- **Rules:** every rule event I checked was correct:
  - Virgin: fired for a Townsfolk nominator (Jules, Kit, Blair); didn't fire for the Drunk (Kit) or the Baron (Harper).
  - Slayer: failed on non-Demons; killed the Imp three times.
  - Scarlet Woman threshold and night order: correct.
- **Cost:** Fable 5 is by far the priciest seat, $0.065 a call; fable5 g1 had cost $32 by day 5. Opus 5.5 was $0.014 a
  call and GPT-6.1 Sol $0.006.

## 4. Overall
The games are realistic and often very good:
- The Spy took on its misregistered Saint.
- The Imp framed the Drunk Monk using the Spy's Grimoire.
- Evil used poisoned Undertaker results (Indigo: "Poisoned Harper last night ... let's see what it says and exploit
  it").
- Scarlet Woman takeovers were reasoned through, and dead players whispered world analyses.

Good still dominates: 6 of 7 finished games. Three of them ended with a day-2 Slayer shot on the Imp, each driven by
information or social reads, not by an engine tell. That is balance, which the Bradley–Terry side term absorbs; it is
not a defect.

**v12 is fit to freeze, with two changes:**
1. **Rule (smallest clean fix):** "A poisoned Fortune Teller's false world has no red herring (only its framed Demon
   says YES)." The Drunk's world keeps its herring for consistency. This raises the poisoned-FT false rate from 60% to
   about 80%, in line with the other roles. Otherwise, state "the red herring is part of every world" so the exception
   is written down.
2. **Documentation:** in PROTOCOL.md, "on 80% of poisoned nights (else true)"; and "an Outsider bluff appears exactly
   when the Drunk is in play".

Items 2.2–2.5 are acceptable Storyteller judgment calls; I recommend no change.
