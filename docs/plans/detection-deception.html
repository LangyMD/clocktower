Clocktower · how the flagship chart would work

Two ratings per model, one fit, and a trend line for each

Every game asks one question: did the good team find the Demon? We fit P(good wins) = σ(detection of the good model − deception of the evil model + h). The side advantage h absorbs good's easier win rate, so a deception rating is not dragged down because evil wins less overall. Opponents are accounted for too.

The headline number: how fast each skill improves

Elo points gained per year, from the trend lines, with 95% intervals. The simulated truth was detection +300 a year and deception +170 a year.

Detection minus deception, both labs pooled: +105 Elo a year (95% interval -114 to +414). With 310 games the interval includes zero, so the run couldn't say which skill is improving faster unless the gap is large.

Why this is fair, and its one caveat

  • Side advantage. Good wins about two-thirds of games. That's one number, h, fitted once, so it doesn't penalize anyone's deception rating.
  • Opponent strength. Beating a strong team counts for more than beating a weak one. That's what the fit does, where raw win rates can't.
  • Setup luck. Every matchup is a side-swapped pair on one seed, so the same characters and seats are played both ways.
  • The caveat. The two scales have one free constant between them: shift every detection rating and h together and nothing changes. So "model X is better at detection than deception" depends on a convention (here: the average model is 1500 on both). But slopes over time, and comparisons within each chart, don't depend on it. The question "is deception improving faster than detection?" is well defined.

Making it precise enough

  • More games. The interval shrinks with the square root of the number of games. Halving it takes four times the games (about $20k).
  • A narrower question. Fit one trend per skill across both labs, rather than per lab. That gives roughly twice the precision on the headline.
  • Probe scores as a second measure. How much probability good seats put on the real Demon, and how little the town puts on evil's Demon, carry information in every game, not just its winner.
  • Focus games where they're informative. Close matchups teach the most. Phase 2 top-ups can target them.
