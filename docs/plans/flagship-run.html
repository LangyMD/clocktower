The flagship run: 21 models, May 2024 to September 2026

Every model still served that was its lab's best at release plays Blood on the Clocktower against the models released near it. Each game rates one model's detection (playing the good team, finding the Demon) against another's deception (playing evil, hiding it). The result is two Elo ratings per model, plotted against release date for Anthropic and OpenAI.

How a game works

  • One model plays every good seat, another every evil seat. 10 to 12 players by seed: two Minions, the Imp, and 7 to 9 good players. A scripted Storyteller runs the night; no LLM judges anything.
  • Each day: 6 private beats (each player may send one whisper per turn, to one or two players, unseen by everyone else), then 3 beats of public talk, then nominations and votes. Every day runs the same length. (In calibration, 8 private beats gave deeper conversations but the same outcomes, for 25% more cost per day.)
  • Equal settings: every model at its lab's "high" effort, one 8K-token ceiling per turn (a turn that hits it retries once at low effort), and the same typography for all speech so no model's punctuation gives it away.
  • Side-swapped pairs. Every matchup is 2 games on one seed: the same characters and seats, with model A good and then model B good. Setup luck cancels out.
  • Memory is each model's own conversation. Eight older models only see their earlier replies, not their earlier reasoning (marked replies only below), which is part of what they're rated on.

Who plays whom

Every pair of models at most 10 places apart in release order plays one side-swapped pair. Far-apart games are nearly certain wins for the newer model and carry little information, so this band is about 10% sharper than a full round robin with a quarter fewer games. Models in the middle of the timeline play 40 games; the two ends play 20.

  • Two models are over a quarter of the bill. o1 (~$6.90 a seat-game) and Opus 4.1 (~$7) are the most expensive by far: their own seats cost about $1k and $1.3k. Dropping them would leave a hole in 2024–25.
  • Wall clock: about half a day. Every game launches at once. Most take 30 minutes to 2 hours; the slowest models (GPT-5.1, GPT-5.4, o3) take up to 3–4 hours. Games checkpoint every night, so a container restart costs only the current day.
  • Logs go to the Hugging Face dataset every 30 minutes; configs and results go to git.

Phase 2: top-ups

After phase 1, fit the ratings, then add side-swapped pairs where they shrink uncertainty most: models close in rating with wide intervals, and the two ends of the timeline. The rule and the budget are fixed in advance and allocate by uncertainty only, never by which way results lean, so the ratings stay unbiased.

Before launch

  1. GPT-5.1's ceiling. About 12% of its turns hit the 8K ceiling (it re-derives the whole game each turn because it can't see its earlier reasoning), and the retry thinks only briefly. Accept that as part of the model, or give it a higher ceiling (32K) as an exception.
  2. Phase 2 budget, fixed now so it can't be steered by results.
